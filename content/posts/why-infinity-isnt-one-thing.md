Cantor's diagonal argument is one of those proofs that feels like a magic trick the first time you see it — and then, once you understand why it works, feels obvious in hindsight.

## The setup

Most people assume "infinity" is a single size. It isn't. The set of natural numbers (1, 2, 3, ...) is infinite, and so is the set of real numbers between 0 and 1. But Cantor showed these two infinities are not the same size — the reals are strictly larger.

![A diagram illustrating countable vs uncountable infinity](/images/posts/infinity-cover.jpg)

## Why it matters

This isn't just a curiosity. It underpins huge parts of modern mathematics and computer science, including why some problems are provably impossible to solve algorithmically.
